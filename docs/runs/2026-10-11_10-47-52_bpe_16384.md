## Observations

At approximately the same 81.92M sampled source-byte budget, this run achieved the lowest recorded best/final BPB among the four BPE vocabulary sizes: 0.6494 / 0.6508, versus 0.6519 / 0.6523 for [8,192](2026-10-11_10-11-29_bpe_8192.md). That small difference comes with higher training time (765.10s versus 705.49s) and peak memory (5,658.3 versus 4,562.4 MiB). Aggregate generation throughput was similar: 121.85 versus 122.54 decoded UTF-8 bytes/sec. These single runs do not establish the significance of the BPB difference.

The training invocation took 2,182.58s in total, with repeated Hugging Face request timeouts during validation-data loading. Those retries are preserved below and excluded from the 765.10s training-step measurement.

The byte baseline's best token loss of 0.5583 corresponds to approximately 0.8055 BPB (derived from rounded logged loss). Compare BPB across tokenizers, rather than token cross-entropy. BPE changes source-byte context coverage and vocabulary parameters; random token-window validation samples also differ. These are single-seed sampled validation measurements.

All five greedy samples exhibit repetition and/or restart with another story. Lower validation BPB does not eliminate these generation issues.

### Historical advisory notes (not verified conclusions)

The source of this conversational advice was not recorded. It is preserved as
historical context, including stronger claims than these single-seed measurements
establish. Recommendations refer to that pre-EOS experiment, not today's defaults.

````markdown
These results give a much clearer picture. The vocabulary sweep is basically complete.

| Vocab | Bytes/token | Context ≈ bytes | Params | Steps | Best BPB | Train time | VRAM |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2048 | 3.6027 | 922 | 59.99M | 2776 | 0.6636 | 738.6s | 3.89 GB |
| 4096 | 3.9351 | 1007 | 61.56M | 2541 | 0.6558 | **696.2s** | 4.03 GB |
| 8192 | 4.0960 | 1049 | 64.71M | 2441 | 0.6519 | 705.5s | 4.56 GB |
| 16384 | 4.1288 | 1057 | 71.01M | 2422 | **0.6494** | 765.1s | 5.66 GB |

The trend is very clear:

```text
2048   0.6636
4096   0.6558   improvement 0.0078
8192   0.6519   improvement 0.0039
16384  0.6494   improvement 0.0025
```

But look at compression:

```text
2048  → 3.6027 bytes/token
4096  → 3.9351   +9.2%
8192  → 4.0960   +4.1%
16384 → 4.1288   +0.8%
```

That last jump is especially revealing. Going from **8192 → 16384 doubles the vocabulary**, but gains only about **0.8% token compression**.

At the same time:

```text
Parameters: 64.7M → 71.0M
VRAM:       4.56GB → 5.66GB
time:       705s → 765s
BPB:        .6519 → .6494
```

So **16384 technically wins quality**, but it's a poor efficiency trade.

### I would choose 8192 as our default

For what you're trying to build—good quality on a local GPU—**8192 looks like the sweet spot**.

4096 is also attractive if maximizing speed/VRAM efficiency, but 8192 buys another measurable quality improvement and still fits very comfortably on the 3060.

I don't think testing 32768 makes much sense. The tokenizer is already saturating: `8192 → 16384` barely increased bytes/token.

### And now we can fairly look at repetition

This is more interesting.

All three equal-budget models show it:

- **4096:** extreme `"I want to go on the slide"` and box loops.
- **8192:** `"he was brave"` / tree repetition.
- **16384:** `"I was just a dog"` and `"I love my ball"` loops.

So we can now say with much more confidence:

> **The repetition problem is not primarily caused by BPE vocabulary size.**

Increasing vocab all the way to 16384 doesn't solve it.

And there is another important variable: we're using **pure greedy decoding**.

Greedy generation means:

```text
at every token:
    pick argmax(probabilities)
```

Once the model falls into a locally self-reinforcing sequence like:

```text
"I want to go on the slide"
```

the same continuation can remain the highest-probability choice repeatedly. There's no mechanism for escaping it.

Therefore, before changing the network architecture to fix repetition, I would make the **next experiment decoding**.

Keep greedy as the deterministic benchmark, but add something like:

```text
--temperature 0.8
--top-p 0.9
```

and perhaps:

```text
--seed 42
```

Then run exactly the same five prompts on the **8192 checkpoint**.

I would **not add repetition penalties yet**. Temperature/top-p is standard probabilistic decoding; repetition penalties actively distort the model distribution and would make it harder to understand what the network actually learned.

So our progression would now be:

```text
Architecture fixed
      ↓
MoE sweep       ✓  → 16 experts / top-2
      ↓
Tokenizer       ✓  → BPE
      ↓
Vocab sweep     ✓  → 8192 sweet spot
      ↓
Decoding        ← NEXT
      ↓
next architecture experiment
```

The vocabulary experiment gave us a useful concrete result: **set `bpe_vocab_size = 8192` and move on rather than squeezing another 0.0025 BPB out of a much larger vocabulary.**
````
