## Model and training

Vocabulary: 256 byte values, with a learned byte embedding and an untied linear next-byte output head. Bias-free QKV/output projections; causal scaled dot-product attention.

Each unique block applies pre-normalization, causal attention with a residual connection, then pre-normalization and the feed-forward network with another residual connection. The sequence of 3 unique blocks is traversed 2 time(s), sharing weights between passes. A final RMSNorm precedes the output head.

TinyStories: `train[:5%]` for training; `validation[:1%]` for validation. Stories joined with newlines and encoded as UTF-8 bytes.

Training uses random contiguous chunks, next-byte cross-entropy, and AdamW with learning rate `3e-4` (other optimizer settings use PyTorch defaults). The random seed is 42. Every 500 steps, averaged over 20 random batches; best checkpoint selected by validation loss.

Generation uses greedy argmax over the last context window, decoding UTF-8 with replacement for invalid byte sequences. Best and final checkpoints are saved as `best_model.pt` and `last_model.pt`; generation reloads the best checkpoint.

The following notes are preserved from this revision. Comparisons may quote rounded figures or suggest future experiments; the configuration and terminal results above describe the implemented run.

### heads=4 -> heads 6

The next architecture experiment I’d do is number of attention heads, because with dim=384, heads=4 means each head is quite large:
384 / 4 = 96 dimensions per head

I’d test heads=6 next:
384 / 6 = 64 dimensions per head

That’s a very common head size and gives attention more independent channels without changing the overall model width much.

| Heads | Head dim | Time | Best val |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 4 | 96 | 658s | 0.7280 |
| **6** | **64** | **629s** | **0.7222** |

Same parameter count and VRAM, but slightly better quality and faster training. So I’d keep 6 heads.

Best as of now with 10000 training steps, random batches
