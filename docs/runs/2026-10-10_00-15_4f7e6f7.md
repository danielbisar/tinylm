## Model and training

Vocabulary: 256 byte values, with a learned byte embedding and an untied linear next-byte output head. PyTorch MultiheadAttention with causal mask and biased projections.

Each unique block applies pre-normalization, causal attention with a residual connection, then pre-normalization and the feed-forward network with another residual connection. The sequence of 4 unique blocks is traversed 1 time(s), sharing weights between passes. A final LayerNorm precedes the output head.

TinyStories: `train[:1%]` for training; `validation[:1%]` for validation. Stories joined with newlines and encoded as UTF-8 bytes.

Training uses random contiguous chunks, next-byte cross-entropy, and AdamW with learning rate `3e-4` (other optimizer settings use PyTorch defaults). No explicit random seed is set. Final validation: 100 random single-sequence samples.

Generation uses greedy argmax over the last context window, decoding UTF-8 with replacement for invalid byte sequences. No checkpoint saving is implemented.

## Historical experiment notes

The following notes are preserved from this revision. Comparisons may quote rounded figures or suggest future experiments; the configuration and terminal results above describe the implemented run.

### dim=384, context=128, recurrent_steps=2, blocks=3

| Design | Params | VRAM | Val loss |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 2 blocks ×3, dim 384 | 3.80M | 99 MB | 1.0384 |
| 3 blocks ×2, dim 384 | 5.57M | 126 MB | **1.0097** |
| 2 blocks ×3, dim 512 | 6.63M | 150 MB | 1.0321 |

So adding more unique blocks helped more than just increasing width.
The generation also looks cleaner.
That suggests our current bottleneck is more likely unique model capacity, not extra recurrent depth.

### dim=384, context=128, recurrent_steps=1, blocks=4

That gives only 4 block applications instead of 6, but more unique parameters. It will tell us whether recurrence is actually buying us anything at this scale.

| Design | Params | Block applications | Time | VRAM | Val loss |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 3 unique ×2 | 5.57M | 6 | 218.6s | 126 MB | **1.0097** |
| 4 unique ×1 | 7.34M | 4 | 173.3s | 158 MB | 1.0239 |

So the 3×2 model is:
- ~24% fewer parameters
- ~20% less VRAM
- slightly better validation loss
- but ~26% slower

For your RTX 3060, that trade is attractive because we have much more compute headroom than VRAM pressure.
I’d keep 3 unique blocks ×2 as our current baseline.
The next thing I’d change is probably the training setup rather than architecture: use a batch larger than 1. Right now each update learns from only one 128-byte sequence, which makes those training-loss numbers jump around a lot and wastes GPU parallelism.
